Your #1 question, answered

“How do I know it's not spyware?”

A fair question — you're linking your WhatsApp. So don't take our word for it. Scry is built so you can check for yourself that nothing shady is going on.

1. No server ever sees your chats

Scry has no cloud and no account. Your messages and media are written straight to your PC. The internet is touched exactly once — to check your license key when you activate — and that request carries only the key and an anonymous machine ID. Never a single byte of your WhatsApp content.

2. Watch where it talks

Open any network monitor while Scry runs. You'll see it connect to WhatsApp's own servers — the exact connection WhatsApp Web needs to be your linked device — and to nothing else of ours. Pull your internet and the app still opens your saved vault: proof it lives on your disk, not in a cloud.

3. It's just files you own

Everything lives in a plain folder on your disk — a local database plus your media. Open it, back it up, or delete it whenever you want. Uninstall Scry and it's all gone. Nothing is ever synced anywhere.

4. WhatsApp's own protocol — revocable

Scry links exactly like WhatsApp Web: the linked-device feature you already use. You stay in control — remove it any time from WhatsApp → Linked devices and it's cut off instantly. No password is ever asked for; WhatsApp doesn't have one.

5. It ignores the rest of your device

Unlike free "status savers," Scry never reads your notifications, your other apps, or your files. It speaks only WhatsApp. Your banking codes, your email, your photos — untouched.

6. Scan it with your own antivirus

You're welcome to check the installer with the antivirus you already trust before running it. One heads-up: because Scry isn't code-signed yet, unsigned software like ours can trip a heuristic “false positive” on a few engines — the same “unverified publisher” reason Windows warns you. It isn't a detection of anything real, and code signing (on our roadmap) clears it up.

Why Windows shows “unknown publisher”

When you run the installer, Windows SmartScreen may pop up “Windows protected your PC.” Read it closely: that warning is about the publisher not being verified yet — not about the file being harmful. Windows shows it for every new independent app that hasn't yet bought a Microsoft-recognized code-signing certificate.

Those certificates aren't cheap — a few hundred dollars a year, plus a registered business identity and a vetting process — so as a small independent product we haven't purchased one yet. We will: buying the trusted Microsoft signature so this warning disappears is on our roadmap as Scry grows. Until then, just click More info → Run anyway — and scan the file first if you'd like (see #6 above).

Questions

Will the other person know?

No. Scry links to your own account like WhatsApp Web, and Ghost mode keeps you invisible — no blue ticks, never shown online, and unseen in status viewers.

How do I know it's not spyware or stealing my data?

Don't trust us — verify. Scry has no cloud: your chats are written straight to a folder on your PC and never uploaded. The only time it uses the internet is a one-off license check at activation, which sends just your key and an anonymous machine ID. Cut your Wi-Fi (or watch your network traffic) and you'll see it talks only to WhatsApp's own servers, exactly like WhatsApp Web. And unlike free status savers, it never touches your notifications or other apps. Full details in the Is it safe? section.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

SmartScreen's “unknown publisher” message means the publisher isn't verified yet — not that the file is harmful. It shows for every new indie app without a Microsoft-recognized code-signing certificate (a few hundred dollars a year plus a business vetting process). We haven't bought one yet, but it's on our roadmap. For now, click More info → Run anyway, and scan the file first if you like.

Why pay when there are free status savers?

Free apps only copy a file if it's already sitting on that phone at that exact second — so view-once only after you open it, deleted messages as plain notification text (no media), and nothing if your timing is off. To do that they demand access to all your notifications and files. Scry captures the real thing over WhatsApp's protocol — before you open it, with media, and even syncs what arrived while your PC was off — without ever reading the rest of your device.

Do I need to be online?

To capture, yes — Scry is your WhatsApp linked device, so it needs internet to receive messages, exactly like WhatsApp Web. Everything else is local: your captured vault is stored on your PC and opens with no connection, and after the one-time activation Scry never contacts our servers again — no telemetry, no re-checks, nothing uploaded. The only place it ever sends data is WhatsApp itself. And if your PC was off when something arrived, Scry catches up the next time it connects.

Which Windows do I need?

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). The installer is about 9 MB and there are no other requirements.

Can I move it to another PC?

One license activates one PC. Changing machines? Just email support and we'll release your key so you can re-activate on the new one.

Is this made by WhatsApp?

No. Scry is independent software and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to WhatsApp or Meta. Use it with your own account, at your own discretion.
